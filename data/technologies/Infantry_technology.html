      Infantry technology unlocks new infantry equipment, motorized equipment, and mechanized equipment, as well as improving infantry and cavalry performance as a whole. It also hosts technologies that enable and improve special forces infantry, as well as an additional set of technologies enhancing special forces without Arms Against Tyranny.

      Some of these technologies, mainly the ones affecting motorized and mechanized equipment, are interlinked with special projects, providing breakthrough progress in land warfare projects and benefitting from any scientist engaged in passive research in that category. Such technologies have a green ribbons in the lower right-hand corner of their icons.

      Weapons & Equipment[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      Infantry weapons[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      This is a community maintained wiki. If you spot a mistake, please help with fixing it.
      Back to Infantry research tree

      Representing an infantryman's standard kit of tools and weapons, Infantry Equipment is the basis of any army. The  Basic Infantry equipment technology starts researched for all countries, while all major powers and many minors have the  Infantry Equipment I technology researched. As infantry equipment serves as the primary, potentially only, source of stats for most unit types, unlocking and fielding upgraded versions of them earlier than the enemy can provide a significant advantage.

      Later technologies also provide infantry with decent hard attack and a small amount of piercing. Although Support Anti-air and/or Support Anti-tank companies, even with older equipment, will still provide infantry divisions with far more piercing, especially because 40%[1] of a division's piercing comes from the brigade or support company with the highest amount, the additional hard attack can still make these divisions far more effective against harder opponents, like tanks or mechanized infantry.

      Support weapons[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      Back to Infantry research tree

      Support weapons includes crew-served weapons such as medium and heavy machine guns and mortars. These technologies provide bonuses to the defense and breakthrough to all types of infantry and to cavalry. This reduces incoming damage taken in combat against enemies who have greater soft and/or hard attack (dependent on the given units' hardness), though it provides little to no additional benefit against enemies with lower attack values.

      Night vision[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      This is a community maintained wiki. If you spot a mistake, please help with fixing it.
      Back to Infantry research tree

      Night vision equipment allows soldiers to see even in the dark, offsetting some of the -50%[2] penalty that units receive when fighting at night, giving an edge over enemies who do not have such equipment. When combined with the  Night Assault Tactics land doctrine, they can potentially remove all night fighting penalties altogether.

      Mobile Infantry[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      This is a community maintained wiki. If you spot a mistake, please help with fixing it.
      Back to Infantry research tree

      Mobile Infantry technology enables Motorized Infantry and Mechanized Infantry, as well as motorized versions of artillery brigades, motorized support companies, and other, more specialized motorized brigades.

      All mobile infantry technologies are linked to land warfare special projects. They provide breakthrough progress in that category when they are being researched, and they benefit from any scientist engaged in passive research in that category.

      Motorized[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      This is a community maintained wiki. If you spot a mistake, please help with fixing it.

      Motorized versions of line brigades tend to be much faster than their non-motorized counterparts, have slightly more hardness, and be slightly better on the attack thanks to having more breakthrough, but they also tend to be more expensive, be less reliable, have more penalties on rugged terrain, and require fuel.

      In addition to units, motorized technology enables and can enhance motorized logistics. Motorized supply extends the range of supply hubs and naval bases, allowing them to supply units that are much further away. However, this comes at the cost of motorized equipment, both when enabling motorization and when said motorized equipment suffers losses from breakdowns in rugged terrain and/or bad weather and/or losses from enemy logistics strikes.

      Mechanized[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      This is a community maintained wiki. If you spot a mistake, please help with fixing it.

      Mechanized technologies focus on improving Mechanized units and can benefit from motorized technology bonuses. Compared to Motorized Infantry, Mechanized Infantry are slower, more expensive, and more fuel-intensive (though they are still much faster than ordinary Infantry), but they have far greater hardness and provide a lot more defense and HP. Their production costs can also be potentially through designing a variant using Army experience, which, when coupled with how few equipment losses they suffer thanks to their high HP, can actually make them more affordable in practice than Motorized Infantry once the combat casualties start rolling in. Although their offensive stats lack compared to tanks', with enough upgrades, Mechanized Infantry can also be surprisingly capable on the offense and can even go toe-to-toe with enemy tanks when used alongside Motorized anti-tank units.

      Amphibious Tractors[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      This is a community maintained wiki. If you spot a mistake, please help with fixing it.

      Amphibious Tractors or Amtracs are special forces versions of Mechanized Infantry more suited to wet terrain and naval invasions, and as special forces, are subject to a country's special forces cap. Although they have less breakthrough than their mechanized counterparts, consume a lot more fuel, and cannot be customized using Army experience to discount them in the same way, amtracs not only receive terrain bonuses in  Marshes,  Rivers, and  Amphibious landings, but they also benefit from special forces attack bonuses from, e.g., special forces doctrines and Commando military high command.

      These technologies can also benefit from both motorized and mechanized technology bonuses, as well as technology bonuses for naval transports.

      Armored Cars[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      This is a community maintained wiki. If you spot a mistake, please help with fixing it.
      Available only with the La Résistance DLC enabled.

      Armored Cars and their Light Armored Recon counterparts are avaialble with La Résistance. These are effectively weaker, softer, much faster, and slightly cheaper alternatives to Light Tanks and Armored Recon, respectively. In theory, their niche is would be for somewhat of a hybrid role between light tanks and motorized infantry, though players may struggle to find a practical use for this niche.

      With No Step Back, they unfortunately suffer from being much less customizable than their tank-based counterparts. Thanks to the tank designer, once their Light Tank counterparts have become obsolete later in the game, they can get up-gunned and/or repurposed into Tank Destroyers, Self-Propelled Artillery, or Self-Propelled Anti-Air, whereas obselete armored car upgrades are limited to incremental variants where they are stuck with their outdated equipment forever. With Arms Against Tyranny, they further suffer from requiring their own, dedicated military industrial organizations that cannot be used for any other type of equipment, whereas their tank-based counterparts share their military industrial organizations with those that can also be used for other types of tanks, thereby offering industrial synergies that armored cars lack.

      These technologies can also benefit from motorized and armor technology bonuses.

      Special forces[modifier | modifier le wikicode]

      This is a community maintained wiki. If you spot a mistake, please help with fixing it.
      Back to Infantry research tree
      See also: Special forces doctrine

      In addition to regular, motorized, and mechanized infantry, there are three specialized infantry types, known as Special Forces or SF (not to be confused with the land doctrine Superior Firepower): Marines, Mountaineers, and Paratroopers. These units are useful in specific situations. They only require standard infantry equipment, but tend to require more of it compared to Infantry.

      Amphibious Tractor and Amphibious Tank units also count as special forces units. The former are closer to Mechanized units than to Infantry and require their own, specialized motorized equipment. The latter are armored units that rely on armor technology. With No Step Back, they can also be built out of Light, Medium, and Heavy chasses using the tank designer.

      Each country has a special forces cap that dictates the maximum amount of special forces battalions they are allowed to field. This cap is the greater of either 24[3] battalions or 5%[4] of the total amount of non-SF battalions making up the rest of your deployed divisions. Divisions cannot be changed, trained, given a different template or have their template adjusted if that would result in exceeding the SF cap. By default, to increase the SF cap beyond the default 24 without any bonuses, you would have to exceed the 480 non-SF battalion breakeven point by 20 for each additional SF battalion. For example, you would need 500 non-SF battalions for 25 SF battalions, 520 non-SF battalions for 26 SF battalions, 540 non-SF battalions for 27 SF battalions, etc. These battalions do not need to be fully equipped or be at full strength, they just need to be part of divisions that are deployed in the field.

      Special forces cap can also be increased through certain political advisors, military high command posts, national focuses, and decisions that affect national spirits. With Arms Against Tyranny, the amount of special forces that can be fielded can be increased through special forces doctrines, both by increasing special forces cap and by decreasing the contribution of certain units to that cap. Without Arms Against Tyranny, special forces cap can be increased with the  Expanded Special Forces Program Special Forces Concentration technology.
